FRIMS

AI-based detection and classification of industrial fires and persistent thermal sources
using NASA FIRMS, OpenStreetMap and satellite land cover

Smart India Hackathon · Problem Statement 26162 · National Technical Research Organisation (NTRO) · Theme: Disaster Management

The problem

"Current satellite-based fire monitoring systems such as NASA FIRMS provide thermal anomaly detections but do not distinguish between industrial fires, gas flares, agricultural burning, mining activity and wildfires."

Deliverable i

Classify and segregate industrial fires from forest and other natural fires.

Deliverable ii

A GIS solution for storage and visualisation of the output as an overlay over maps.

Problem2

Why it is hard: every hot dot looks the same

What FIRMS gives

A point, a time, brightness, fire power, day/night — four passes a day at 375 m.

What it does not say

Whether it is a stubble fire in Punjab, a wildfire in Uttarakhand, the Jamnagar flare, or Jharia's century-old coal fire.

Our observation

Nature does not burn in the same spot night after night for weeks. Industry does.

Problem3

Our idea: ask three questions about every detection

1 · What is under it?

ESA WorldCover 10 m land cover: cropland, forest, bare, built-up, water.

2 · What is next to it?

44,381 industrial footprints: refineries, steel, power, GEM coal mines, LNG, offshore fields, NASA/EOG Nightfire flares, OSM industrial areas.

3 · Does it keep happening?

Per 1 km cell and per site: active days in the last 30 / 90 days, night fraction, usual fire power.

A transparent rule checklist turns the answers into seven classes; an independent ML model trained on NASA's own labels gives a second opinion.

The rule checklist (first match wins)

#EvidenceClass
1inside a known volcanovolcano
2refinery / oil-gas / LNG site · mostly night · hot ≥ 10 of last 90 daysgas flare
3coalfield / mine / quarry · persistentmining thermal
4any named industrial site · persistentindustrial – persistent source
5inside a named site but not persistent — or hotter than the site ever is (≥ 4σ)industrial fire — ALERT
6persistent, no site on recordunmapped persistent source
7cropland · daytime · seen ≤ 3 daysagricultural burning

Every threshold is one number in one config file. Every label carries a plain-English explanation ("coalfield site Jharia at 0 m; 54 active days / 90; night fraction 0.77; land cover grassland").

Classification6

Validated against NASA's own answer key

NASA's slower standard-processing product tags each detection: vegetation fire or static land source. We never train on our own rules.

  • Truth: 2.8 M detections, two VIIRS sensors, Aug 2025 → May 2026
  • Rules tuned + ML trained on Nov–Dec 2025
  • Tested on Jan–May 2026 — 1.49 M detections it never saw
  • ML = gradient-boosted trees on 21 features; strongest signals: active days, night fraction, site persistence

We find ~98 % of confirmed industrial sources; ~19 % of our "industrial" calls are transient fires near industrial land — the cost we chose to pay for recall. NASA's own labels are inconsistent in a quarter of those cells, so the ceiling is ≈ 92 %.

Results8

Tested on real incidents — including the misses

14 news-reported industrial incidents (Nov 2025 – Aug 2026), each with a source link, geocoded and checked against what the satellite saw.

8 / 14seen from space
1 + 1alert raised · watch-tier hit
  • ONGC Mori-5 well blowout, Konaseema, Jan 2026 — burned five days: 14 night detections, all flagged industrial fire.
  • Seen, below alert grade: Vizag Steel ladle explosion (19.8 MW), Mumbai High platform fire (caught by the watch tier) — inside complexes that are always hot.
  • Invisible: six short daytime or indoor events — 375 m pixels, four passes a day.

Lesson we act on: the site database, not the detector, limits alert recall.

Engineering11

From India to the world

Scale numbers

  • Global: 100–500 k detections/day, 50–150 M/year
  • Our pipeline: one day of global data in ~15 s; a year in ~1 h
  • Storage: ~40–60 GB/year

Same code, production plumbing

  • PostGIS + incremental daily rollups
  • Global Energy Monitor + VIIRS Nightfire flare catalogue as the site layer
  • Vector tiles + MapLibre; workers + Redis cache
  • On-prem / air-gapped deployable

Honest limits

  • FIRMS latency ~3 h; geostationary (INSAT-3D / MSG-IODC) needs a direct feed
  • 375 m pixels miss short or indoor fires
  • Alerts inside always-hot plants need per-source adaptive thresholds — to be validated on new incidents
  • Site database completeness is the lever
Scale & next12

Summary

  • i Industrial vs natural segregation: 98 % recall, 89 % F1 against NASA's independent labels on 1.5 M unseen detections; seven explainable classes plus an independent ML opinion.
  • ii GIS storage (GeoPackage, opens in QGIS) and an interactive overlay with persistent-source registry, alerts, playback and offline mode.
  • Validated on real incidents, with the misses reported, not hidden.
  • Runs on a laptop today; a clear path to a global, on-prem deployment.
